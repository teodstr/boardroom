# Business Model Canvas

Map how a business works on one page, check that the blocks fit together, and find the assumptions that could break it.

## When to use

- Designing a new venture or a business plan, or explaining "how does X make money?".
- Comparing two business models, or looking for a new one (subscription, marketplace, freemium…).
- For the profitability of one customer or one unit, continue with `unit-economics`.

## Inputs

1. The business (existing or planned), its offer and its customers.
2. Any known figures: prices, volumes, main costs.

## Method

1. **Start with the customer side:**
   - **Customer segments:** who exactly (not "everyone"). One canvas per very different segment if needed.
   - **Value propositions:** the problem solved or the gain created for each segment.
   - **Channels:** how customers discover, buy and receive the offer.
   - **Customer relationships:** self-service, personal assistance, community, automated…
   - **Revenue streams:** what customers pay for and how (sale, subscription, commission, licensing, advertising), with price levels.
2. **Then the infrastructure:**
   - **Key resources:** what the model cannot work without.
   - **Key activities:** what the company must do well.
   - **Key partners:** who provides what the company does not do itself.
   - **Cost structure:** the main costs, fixed and variable.
3. **Check the fit:** each value proposition serves a segment, each revenue stream matches a segment's willingness to pay, the activities deliver the propositions, and the costs follow from the activities and resources.
4. **Zoom on the value proposition** if it is weak (Value Proposition Canvas): list the segment's jobs, pains and gains, then the products, pain relievers and gain creators. Keep only the strongest matches.
5. **Find the riskiest assumptions** on desirability (do they want it?), feasibility (can we do it?) and viability (does it make money?), and propose a cheap test for each.
6. **For an existing company,** say what makes the model hard to copy and where it is fragile.

## Quality bar

- Segments are specific and every block is concrete (names, channels, price levels).
- Revenue streams state the pricing mechanism.
- The blocks are consistent with each other, and inconsistencies are flagged.
- The analysis ends with risks and tests, or a recommendation.
- No invented figures: label estimates as assumptions.
- Written in the user's language, with the standard terms below.
