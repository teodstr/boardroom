# Segmentation, targeting, positioning (STP)

Split the market into groups that behave differently, choose the ones to serve, and define a position the brand can own in their minds.

## When to use

- Launching a product or brand, repositioning, or answering "who is our customer?".
- Before `marketing-mix`: the mix should follow from the target and the positioning.

## Inputs

1. The offer and the market.
2. Any customer data: surveys, sales by customer type, reviews, interviews.
3. The main competitors and how they present themselves.

## Method

1. **Segment.** Pick variables that explain differences in needs or behavior:
   - Consumers: demographic, geographic, psychographic (lifestyle, values), behavioral (usage, benefits sought, loyalty).
   - Businesses: size, industry, buying process, needs.
   Build 3–6 segments that are measurable, large enough, reachable, distinct and actionable.
2. **Profile each segment:** size, growth, needs, willingness to pay, current alternatives.
3. **Target.** Score each segment on attractiveness (size, growth, profitability, competition) and on the company's ability to serve it. Choose a targeting strategy: undifferentiated, differentiated (several segments), concentrated (one niche) or one-to-one.
4. **Position.** Choose the frame of reference (the category the brand competes in), the points of parity (what it must match) and the points of difference (what makes it better). Write the positioning statement:
   *For* [target] *who* [need], [brand] *is the* [frame of reference] *that* [key benefit], *because* [reason to believe].
5. **Draw a perceptual map** on the two dimensions the target cares about most. Place competitors, and look for an empty space that a target segment actually values.
6. **Optionally, write one persona per target segment**, built from the data, not from imagination.

## Output

```markdown
### Segments
| Segment | Size | Needs | Willingness to pay | Attractiveness | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Target
<Chosen segment(s) and why.>

### Positioning statement
For … who …, <brand> is the … that …, because ….

### Perceptual map
| Brand | <Axis 1> | <Axis 2> |
|---|---|---|

### Implications for the marketing mix
- …
```

## Quality bar

- Segments differ by needs or behavior, not only by age or income.
- The target choice is justified by attractiveness and fit.
- The positioning statement is specific, credible and backed by a reason to believe.
- The empty space on the map is one the target cares about, not just an empty corner.
- Consumer insights without data are labeled as hypotheses to validate (survey, interviews).
- Written in the user's language, with the standard terms below.
