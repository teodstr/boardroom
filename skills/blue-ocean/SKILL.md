# Blue Ocean Strategy

Stop benchmarking competitors and redesign the offer around what buyers value, so that competition becomes less relevant.

## When to use

- A crowded market where everyone competes on the same factors and prices fall.
- Designing a new concept, or a differentiation strategy for a small player.
- To understand the forces of an existing industry first, use `porter-five-forces`.

## Inputs

1. The industry, its standard offer and its main players.
2. The target buyers, and the non-customers you could attract.

## Method

1. **List the factors of competition:** the 6–10 things the industry competes on (price, range, service, speed, design, location…).
2. **Draw the strategy canvas:** score each main player (and the company) from 1 to 5 on each factor. If the curves look alike, it's a red ocean.
3. **Look beyond current customers:** people who barely use the offer, people who refuse it, and people the industry never considered. Also look at alternative industries that meet the same need, and at whether the industry competes on function or on emotion.
4. **Apply the four actions (ERRC):**
   - **Eliminate** factors the industry takes for granted but buyers don't value.
   - **Reduce** factors well below the industry standard.
   - **Raise** factors well above the industry standard.
   - **Create** factors the industry has never offered.
5. **Draw the new value curve.** A good one has focus, diverges from competitors, and fits in a short, compelling tagline.
6. **Check viability in this order:** exceptional buyer utility, a price the mass of target buyers can pay, a cost structure that delivers a profit at that price, and how to overcome adoption hurdles (partners, employees, the public).

## Output

```markdown
### Strategy canvas — <industry>
| Factor of competition | <Player A> | <Player B> | <Company> | New curve |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### ERRC grid
| Eliminate | Reduce |
|---|---|
| … | … |

| Raise | Create |
|---|---|
| … | … |

### New offer
<Tagline + description of the new value curve.>

### Viability check
| Utility | Price | Cost | Adoption |
|---|---|---|---|
```

## Quality bar

- The factors are those buyers actually care about, and the scores are justified.
- The ERRC moves both cut costs (eliminate, reduce) and add value (raise, create). Differentiation alone is not a blue ocean.
- The new curve clearly differs from competitors' curves and can be summed up in one tagline.
- Non-customers are considered.
- Viability is checked (utility, price, cost, adoption).
- Written in the user's language, with the standard terms below.
