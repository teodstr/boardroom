# Luxury brand strategy

Luxury does not follow the usual marketing rules. This skill checks whether a brand is truly managed as luxury, describes its identity, and finds where it risks dilution.

## When to use

- Luxury and premium brands: fashion and leather goods, watches and jewelry, hospitality, wine and spirits, beauty, cars, yachts.
- "Should this brand go upmarket?", "Is this extension diluting the brand?", "How do we grow without losing desirability?"
- For a general positioning question, use `stp-positioning`.

## Inputs

1. The brand, its products and its price levels compared with competitors.
2. Its history and origin, its distribution (own stores, retailers, online, outlets) and its communication.
3. The question to answer (growth, extension, repositioning, crisis of desirability).

## Method

1. **Classify the brand.**
   - **Luxury:** superlative and not comparative, built on heritage, craftsmanship and rarity. The brand sets its own standards.
   - **Premium:** the best in its category, judged by comparison (quality-price at the top of the market).
   - **Fashion:** driven by trends and seasons, fast renewal.
   Many strategic mistakes come from managing a luxury brand like a premium one.
2. **Map the brand identity prism (six facets):**
   - **Physique:** signature products, materials, colors, logos.
   - **Personality:** the character the brand would have as a person.
   - **Culture:** values, origin, heritage.
   - **Relationship:** the kind of bond with clients (protective, intimate, initiatory…).
   - **Reflection:** the image of the typical client as others see them.
   - **Self-image:** how clients see themselves when they use the brand.
   Physique, relationship and reflection give the brand its social, outward expression; personality, culture and self-image are its inner spirit.
3. **Audit the brand against the rules of luxury management** (adapted from Kapferer and Bastien):
   - **Uniqueness over comparison:** build an identity of its own rather than positioning against competitors.
   - **Price signals value:** prices rise over time, discounts and promotions are avoided, and the entry price protects the dream.
   - **Controlled scarcity:** selective or owned distribution, no chasing every sale, waiting lists accepted.
   - **Communication builds the dream:** it also speaks to people who are not buyers today, through heritage, craftsmanship and stories rather than product claims.
   - **Experience is part of the product:** service rituals, personalization, after-sales care.
   - **Authenticity:** origin and savoir-faire stay credible (where and how products are made matters).
   - **The brand stays the hero:** celebrity partnerships serve the brand and never overshadow it.
4. **Find the dilution risks:** over-extension, licensing, outlets and discounting, overexposure on social media, wholesale channels the brand does not control.
5. **Evaluate any brand extension** (upward, downward, into new categories) by its fit with the prism and its dilution risk.
6. **Recommend how to grow without losing desirability:** new geographies, experiences and hospitality, limited editions, bespoke services, while protecting the core.

## Quality bar

- The classification is argued with evidence (price, distribution, heritage), not assumed from the brand's reputation.
- Each prism facet is specific to the brand and supported by an observable fact.
- The audit names concrete practices (a price increase, an outlet, a collaboration), not principles alone.
- Growth recommendations explain how they protect desirability.
- No invented figures or anecdotes about the brand: label hypotheses.
- Written in the user's language, with the standard terms below.
